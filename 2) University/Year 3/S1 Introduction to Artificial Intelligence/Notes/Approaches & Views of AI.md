## Thinking Humanly
**Mimicking human thought processes**
- Mimicking human thought processes to understand and replicate how humans think
- Trying to build a theory/model of the human mind, which can then be implemented in a computer program

### Cognitive Modelling Approach
- **Introspection** - Trying to catch our own thoughts as they go by
- **Psychological experiments** - Observing a person in action. Knowing if someone is happy, sad, etc.
- **Brain imaging** - Observing the brain in action

### Cognitive Science
The field of cognitive science brings together computer models from AI and experimental techniques from psychology, to better construct precise and testable theories of the human mind

### Challenges and Applications
**Challenges**
Difficulty in accurately modeling modeling human cognition

**Examples**
Cognitive simulation models - Predict how people solve puzzles by mimicking thought patterns

Eliza - An early chatbot that attempted to simulate a human therapist's responses

## Acting Humanly
**Performing tasks indistinguishably from a human**
Requirements is by passing the Turing test

### Turing Test
Proposed by Alan Turning, to evaluate if a machine can exhibit behavior indistinguishable from a human. A computer passes the test if a human interrogator, after posing  
some written questions, cannot tell whether the written  
responses come from a person or from a computer.

#### What is needed?
To pass the turning test, you will need:
- **Natural Language Processing**
  To communicate successfully in a human language
- **Knowledge representation**
  To store what it knows or hears
- **Automated reasoning**
  To answer questions and draw new conclusions
- **Machine learning**
	To adapt to new circumstances, and to detect and extrapolate patterns

#### Total Turing Test
Expanding on the turing test, it requires interaction with objects and people in the real world.

To pass, a robot will need:
- Computer vision and speech recognition
- Robotics to manipulate objects and move about

### Challenges and Applications
**Challenges**
Difficulty in accurately modeling human cognition

**Applications**
Customer service chatbots, virtual assistants

Example:
- ChatGPT
- Siri
  Alexa
  Google Assistant


## Thinking Rationally
**Using formal reasoning and logic**
- Automated theorem proving
### Laws of Thought Approach
- Logic-based AI focuses on deriving conclusions from a set of premises
- For reasoning with uncertain information, probability is used. However, it does not generate intelligent behavior, and is not "rational/logical".

### Examples and Challenges
**Automated Theorem Provers**
Using systems like Prolog, to solve logical problems

**Medical Diagnosis Systems**
Using logical rules to infer diseases, based on symptoms

**Challenges**
Real-world problems often require handing uncertainty, which traditional logic struggles to address

## Acting Rationally
**Taking action to achieve goals efficiently**
- Designing systems that act to achieve the best possible outcomes, given the available information

### Rational Agent Approach
- An agent is something that acts
- A computer agent are expected to do more. Such as operating autonomously, perceiving their environment, persisting over a prolonged time period, adapting to change, and pursuing goals
- A rational agent is one to achieve the best outcome. Or if there is uncertainty, the best expected outcome. They aim to maximize utility or minimize costs in a given situation

### Advantages and Examples
**Advantages of using a rational agent approach, over the other approaches**
It is more general than the laws of thought approach, and is more amenable to scientific development

**AlphaGo** - Combines deep learning and reinforcement learning to play go efficiently
**Self-driving Cars** - Uses rational decision-making to navigate traffic and avoid collisions
